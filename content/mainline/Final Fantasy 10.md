# Significance
Final Fantasy X is often regarded as a turning point in the franchise. The PlayStation 2 used DVD-ROMs instead of CD-ROMs which allowed for a dramatic increase in the amount of data that could be stored on the disk. This allowed Square to introduce full voice acting for the first time in the series. This, coupled with the increased graphical fidelity of the PlayStation 2, allowed the team to really lean into the cinematic experience that they had been flirting with since [[Final Fantasy 06|FF6]]. This entry is generally considered to be the final one to use a traditional, turn-based JRPG battle system. Moving forward, the series would become more detached from its traditional JRPG roots and focus more on spectacle and graphical fidelity. The game was a financial success for the company. So much so that Square felt the need to create the first direct sequel to a mainline entry. While this game brought a lot of attention to the series and created many new fans, some of the old-heads (myself included) consider it the end of the series' golden age.
